# MSK Magazine: Indian retail reader editorial standard

**Owner instruction, 24 November 2026.** The magazine covers **Indian stock markets** and is written for **Indian retail investors**, including readers who may understand Nifty and Bank Nifty but not specialist institutional research language.

## Mandatory writing rules for every issue

1. **Simple Indian financial English** is the default. Say *shares*, *companies*, *profit*, *debt*, *cash flow*, *Nifty*, *Bank Nifty*, *RBI* and *crore*. Use a short explanation on first use of necessary technical terms. Do not assume the reader understands Bloomberg-style terminology.
2. **Keep all facts exact.** A plain-language rewrite must not change figures, units, source dates, fiscal periods, whether an index return is TRI, or a company-reported figure is in INR versus USD. Do not imply a future forecast from an observed past return.
3. **Indian money format:** Prefer `Rs 73,188 crore` (or `₹73,188 crore` if the PDF font embeds the rupee symbol correctly), `₹1 lakh`, `₹10 lakh`, `₹1 crore`; do not silently change a source's $ million figures into rupees using an invented FX rate. If an original source uses $ million, retain it and explain it is in US dollars.
4. **Always name the period.** Use e.g. *October 2026 total return*, not *today's rally*. The monthly issue may be published in November while the official index returns are from October.
5. **Explain why it matters without a tip.** Two compact editorial boxes are useful: `WHAT HAPPENED` (source-backed metric) and `WHAT IT MEANS` (clearly labelled interpretation), plus `RISK / WHAT WE DON'T KNOW`.
6. **Avoid these unexplained phrases:** *equity discount-rate backdrop*, *index concentration proxy*, *heterogeneous earnings*, *cross-currency ranking*, *free-float market capitalization*, *issuer-specific valuation*, *extrapolate a trend*, *conditional stress test*. Replace with accurate everyday language and keep necessary nuance.
7. **Acronyms need help, not deletion.** Use *RBI (Reserve Bank of India)*, *CPI (consumer inflation)*, *IIP (industrial production)*, *P/E (price-to-earnings ratio)*, *MPC (RBI rate-setting committee)*, and *FII/DII (foreign/domestic institutional investors)* when first used in an issue.
8. **No unsupported market claims.** No *all-time high*, *gold crash*, *best five stocks*, *80% winning trades*, or *guaranteed returns* without the appropriate dated original evidence. Do not fabricate support/resistance, candle history, tips or individual-stock rankings.
9. **Keep design readable on a phone.** Avoid tiny footnotes, dense paragraphs, overlong charts, jargon-only titles, or unexplained colour coding. Charts must display index name, observed month, measured percentage, units and source.
10. **Research and compliance:** This is general educational research, not personal advice. Keep clickable original sources, verification checks, issue/date labels and the existing no-publication gate. A new PDF must be visually and financially reviewed again.

## Small glossary for the contents page

- **Nifty 50:** Index of 50 major NSE-listed companies.
- **Bank Nifty:** Index tracking major listed banking shares.
- **P/E:** Share price compared with company earnings; by itself it does not mean cheap or expensive.
- **CPI:** Inflation in prices paid by consumers.
- **Repo rate:** RBI policy rate that influences borrowing rates, but is not directly every customer's loan rate.
- **Basis point (bps):** One-hundredth of a percentage point. 25 bps = 0.25 percentage points.
- **Total return (TRI):** An index measure including reinvested dividends according to the index methodology.
- **Volatility:** How much returns move around; higher volatility generally means larger swings.

## Plain-language example

Instead of: *Market breadth was weak because capitalization-weighted returns diverged from equal-weight performance.*

Write: *In October, the Nifty 500 fell 0.75%, while its equal-weight version fell 5.12%. That means the larger companies held up better than many other companies in the index. This comparison is not an official count of how many shares rose or fell.*
